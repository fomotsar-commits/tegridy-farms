## Part A — Real liquidity & market-making (the honest answer to "volume")

Genuine market-making means putting **real inventory** on both sides of a book, quoting **real spreads**, and carrying **real inventory risk** — the maker profits from spread/fees and loses when price runs against them, and buyers get tighter fills and less slippage as a result. **Wash trading** is the opposite: the same entity (or colluding bots) trades with itself to print a volume number, adds zero real depth, exposes no inventory, and exists only to deceive buyers into thinking a token is liquid. The line is objective — does a non-participant get a better fill because the activity happened? Market-making: yes. Wash trading: no. Everything below is the market-making side.

### The 2026 landscape

**1. Automated LP / concentrated-liquidity management (ALM).** The audited, integratable engines that manage a real concentrated position and rebalance it as price moves:

- **Arrakis PALM / Arrakis V2** — "Protocol Automated Liquidity Management," explicitly built for **protocol-owned liquidity**, MIT-licensed, audited by **WatchPug and Statemind**, rebalanced by **Gelato keepers**. Confirmed **Uniswap V3 only** — no V4 support in that repo ([github.com/ArrakisFinance/v2-palm README](https://github.com/ArrakisFinance/v2-palm/blob/main/README.md), [arrakis.finance/blog/introducing-arrakis-palm](https://arrakis.finance/blog/introducing-arrakis-palm)). This is the closest thing to a fork-ready "honest MM for your own token."
- **Gamma Strategies** — non-custodial ALM, publicly audited vaults, ~$200M+ TVL across Ethereum/Polygon/Arbitrum; "set-and-forget" managed ranges ([consensys.io/blog/gamma-strategies](https://consensys.io/blog/gamma-strategies-an-innovative-solution-to-the-challenge-of-liquidity-management)).
- **Steer Protocol** — general-purpose ALM where you deploy custom logic-driven vaults on any AMM; more flexible, lower TVL than Gamma ([chainscorelabs comparison](https://chainscorelabs.com/comparisons/dex-concentrated-vs-full-range-liquidity/liquidity-management-infrastructure/gamma-vs-steer-protocol)).
- **Maverick** — AMM with directional liquidity "modes" that auto-follow price ([docs.mav.xyz](https://docs.mav.xyz/guides/liquidity-providers/liquidity-strategies)).

All four are *genuine* liquidity when funded with real inventory; they become "mercenary" only when the capital is rented via emissions and leaves the moment incentives stop.

**2. Uniswap V4 hook-based market-making.** V4 treats **LVR** (loss-versus-rebalancing — the value passive LPs bleed to arbitrageurs when the pool price lags a faster venue) as the core problem, and the hook toolkit is what's audited vs. what's still research:
- **Audited / production-usable:** OpenZeppelin's `uniswap-hooks` library ships `LiquidityPenaltyHook` (penalizes JIT add-then-remove within a short block window — the same JIT mitigation your `TegridyV4Hook` already inherits), `AntiSandwichHook` (no fill better than block-open price), and `BaseDynamicFee`/`BaseOverrideFee` (raise fees when price moves sharply → tax arbitrage → hand LVR back to LPs). It was audited by OZ across a Milestone-1 pass and v1.1.0-rc.1/rc.2 rounds; v1.0.0 shipped 2025-02-24, v1.1.0 on 2025-07-11 ([OZ Milestone-1 audit](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/uniswap-hooks-library-milestone-1-audit), [OZ v1.1.0-rc.2 audit](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/openzeppelin-uniswap-hooks-v1.1.0-rc-2-audit), [docs.openzeppelin.com/uniswap-hooks](https://docs.openzeppelin.com/uniswap-hooks)). Caveat: the repo README still carries a generic "not audited / as-is" disclaimer, and rc.2 still had one residual high — pin a fixed, reviewed version ([github.com/OpenZeppelin/uniswap-hooks](https://github.com/OpenZeppelin/uniswap-hooks)).
- **Research-grade, NOT audited/production:** Arrakis's **Diamond** LVR-minimizing hook (joint Arrakis + Conor McMenamin + Uniswap Foundation grant, forces arbitrage to block-open and returns LVR to LPs) is a **proof-of-concept** at `github.com/ArrakisFinance/minimize-lvr-hook-poc` ([Arrakis "Diamond" writeup](https://mirror.xyz/0x929fCf268A62e684221f1e39B8b6ddA2f0dA4AeC/ZhTQJ6qiurBTHBNuF08GudWBQ8x9p3P-12vo-C9czKE)). **Angstrom** (Sorella Labs, per-block uniform-clearing batch auction that returns arb value to LPs) is the other much-watched design — *UNVERIFIED audit/production status, do not rely.* Do not hand-roll either; they re-trigger a full external audit.

**3. Protocol-owned MM / liquidity-as-a-service vs. mercenary incentives.** The honest way to seed and deepen a new pool is a layered stack: (a) **POL** — the protocol buys the token with its own ETH and holds the LP permanently (exactly what your `POLAccumulator` does today: swap half the ETH → token, add V2 LP, never withdraw); (b) an **ALM** managing that POL as an active concentrated position (PALM/Gamma/Arrakis-Pro); (c) **incentivized third-party LPs** via gauges/boosted farming to add depth on top. The critical discipline: emissions *rent* liquidity and it evaporates when incentives stop, so POL must carry the floor — depth that survives incentives-off is the only depth that counts.

**4. Bonding-curve → AMM graduation.** This is where Doppler already does the honest handoff for you. At graduation the **Airlock** hands proceeds to a **migrator module**, and the migrator choice is exactly the honest-vs-shallow dial ([docs.doppler.lol/core-concepts/liquidity-migration-options](https://docs.doppler.lol/core-concepts/liquidity-migration-options.md)):
  - `v2Migrator` → Uniswap V2, **LP not locked**, no fee stream.
  - `v3Migrator` → Uniswap V3, **LP not locked**.
  - `v4Migrator` + **`StreamableFeesLocker`** → Uniswap V4, **LP time-locked** (`lockDuration` configurable, docs example 365 days), **trading fees streamed to beneficiaries**, and the docs state the **"airlock owner must receive at least 5% of streamed fees."**
  - `NoOp` → liquidity stays in the auction contract (not for dynamic auctions).

  The V4 migrator path *is* the honest deepen-and-lock rail: real liquidity, locked, earning a real fee stream. Doppler's contracts were assessed by **Certora (Nov 2024)** and run an ongoing **Cantina** competition ([Certora Doppler report](https://certora.cdn.prismic.io/certora/Z6zPyJbqstJ9-ib-_DopplerSecurityAssessmentReport_Final.pdf), [Cantina competition](https://cantina.xyz/competitions/57b00aab-8f8b-4d62-9378-41b6460ce6aa), [github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler](https://github.com/whetstoneresearch/doppler)).

**5. RFQ / PMM / solver liquidity.** CoW, 0x, 1inch and Hashflow route through two solver types: **AMM-solvers** (best on-chain path) and **PMM-solvers** (professional market makers quoting from **private inventory** via RFQ) ([docs.cow.fi solvers](https://docs.cow.fi/cow-protocol/concepts/introduction/solvers), [Hashflow RFQ](https://phemex.com/academy/what-is-hashflow-hft)). The honest caveat for a *fresh* token: **PMMs do not quote a token until a market maker chooses to onboard it** — so RFQ is not a liquidity *source* you can summon for a day-one launch. What solvers *will* do immediately is route across whatever **on-chain** liquidity exists, which means the lever you control is making the graduated pool deep and standards-compliant so aggregators find and route to it. Tight PMM quotes come later, and via off-chain MM business development, not code.

### Tegridy recommendation (minimal-surface)

Give graduated launches real depth in three layers, each mapped to an existing, audited component — near-zero new Solidity:

1. **Graduate through Doppler's `v4Migrator` + `StreamableFeesLocker`, not the V2 migrator.** This is the single highest-leverage, zero-new-code move: the graduated LP is time-locked and streams real fees. Your `afterlife.ts` already assumes a V4 pool, so this is a **launcher config choice** (migrator selection + `lockDuration` + beneficiary shares at create time), not a contract. Make the protocol/POL a fee-stream beneficiary. *This is honest "volume infrastructure": locked real liquidity, real fee accrual.*

2. **Manage the pool as a real concentrated position with an audited ALM — integrate, don't fork.** For active spread/depth on the graduated pool, prefer **integrating Gamma or an Arrakis V4 vault unmodified** over forking. If you fund it with POL — your `POLAccumulator` ETH plus owner-supplied TOWELI/token inventory — you get genuine two-sided depth with real inventory risk, the antithesis of wash volume. **Hard flag:** Arrakis **PALM is V3-only**; a graduated **V4** pool cannot use PALM as-is. Either (a) run the ALM on a V4-native manager, or (b) keep a paired V3 satellite pool for a PALM-managed position. Confirm a V4-native audited ALM exists before committing (open question below).

3. **Layer incentivized depth on top via the flywheel you already have.** `afterlife.ts` → per-pool `TegridyBoostedLPStaker` + `GaugeController`/`VoteIncentives` + `TegridyLPFarming` are already the audited rails to attract third-party LPs with veTOWELI-boosted emissions. Keep the discipline from your own notes: emissions rent liquidity; POL (layers 1–2) must hold the floor so depth survives incentives-off.

4. **Keep LVR/JIT defense to what's already audited.** The graduated pool's hook should keep **OZ `LiquidityPenaltyHook` (JIT) + dynamic-fee** — exactly what `TegridyV4Hook` already bundles. Use the **dynamic fee as the pragmatic LVR tax**; do **not** hand-roll Diamond/Angstrom-style LVR auctions (research-grade, unaudited).

**Audit-cost triggers to respect:**
- `TegridyV4Hook` is already marked *"NOT YET DEPLOYABLE (HookMiner deploy + audit pending)"* — deploying it, or adding *any* new hook logic, is a full audit.
- **Integrating** an unmodified Gamma/Arrakis vault = integration review only. **Forking and modifying** PALM = re-audit of the diff. Choose integrate.
- Selecting Doppler's migrator + registering fee-stream beneficiaries = **config, no new audit** (but verify migrator address + params on-chain).
- Deploying `TegridyBoostedLPStaker` instances per pool = ops, not new code (contract already audited).
- Any on-chain "manager/rebalancer" wrapper you write around an ALM = new attack surface → audit. Keep rebalancing off-chain (keeper) calling audited contracts.

### Fork-not-build

Graduate via **Doppler's audited `v4Migrator` + `StreamableFeesLocker`** (locked, fee-streaming real LP), manage the POL position with an **integrated audited ALM (Gamma / Arrakis)**, defend it with the **OZ-audited JIT + dynamic-fee hooks you already inherit**, and incentivize the rest through your **existing gauge + boosted-LP flywheel** — write only launcher config and per-pool deploy glue, no novel Solidity.

## Part B — One-click / atomic buys (the honest answer to "bundler")

The legitimate "bundler" is a **one-signature, single-address, fully-disclosed atomic launch-buy** — not a multi-wallet snipe/concealment tool. Tegridy already ships the correct foundation: `frontend/src/lib/eip5792.ts` (FINAL EIP-5792 `wallet_sendCalls`/`getCapabilities`, v2.0.0 envelope, atomic-status gating) with a working approve+stake pattern (`stakeBatch.ts` + `useOneClickStake.ts`). The one-click launch-buy is the same shape with more legs — **zero new Solidity**.

### The AA landscape (2026)

- **EIP-5792 (Wallet Call API) — FINAL.** Defines `wallet_sendCalls` / `wallet_getCallsStatus` / `wallet_showCallsStatus` / `wallet_getCapabilities`; batch envelope version `2.0.0`; per-chain `atomic` capability advertises `supported | ready | unsupported`. Tegridy's `eip5792.ts` already models this (`isAtomicBatchSupported()` reads `capabilities[chainIdHex].atomic.status` and treats `supported`+`ready` as go; `sendCalls()` sends the v2.0.0 envelope with `atomicRequired`), proven end-to-end by `stakeBatch.ts` + `useOneClickStake.ts`. ([eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5792](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5792) · [eip5792.xyz/reference/sendCalls](https://www.eip5792.xyz/reference/sendCalls) · [docs.metamask.io — send-batch-transactions](https://docs.metamask.io/wallet/how-to/send-transactions/send-batch-transactions/))
- **Wallet support is broad enough to ship one-click as primary-when-available.** As of 2026 the atomic-batch path is live in MetaMask (via an in-line EIP-7702 EOA upgrade), Coinbase Smart Wallet, Rainbow, Trust, Safe (via WalletConnect), thirdweb in-app wallets; Ledger and others testing. MetaMask's flow: on `wallet_sendCalls` it prompts a one-time EIP-7702 upgrade to a MetaMask smart account, then executes the batch as one atomic tx (one confirmation) — exactly Tegridy's `atomic.status==='ready'` branch. WalletConnect Certified requires EIP-5792, pushing adoption. The capability-gated posture (offer one-click only when advertised, else keep the proven sequential flow) is correct. ([docs.metamask.io — batch-transactions](https://docs.metamask.io/metamask-connect/evm/guides/send-transactions/batch-transactions/) · [walletconnect.com — EIP-5792 UX](https://walletconnect.com/blog/eip-5792-the-ux-breakthrough-everyone-s-ignoring) · [blog.thirdweb.com — EIP-5792 guide](https://blog.thirdweb.com/what-is-eip-5792-a-guide-to-ethereums-wallet-call-api/))
  > **Note:** only MetaMask's 7702-upgrade + single-confirmation flow was independently confirmed by the verifier; the broader wallet list is consistent with 2026 adoption but treat individual entries beyond MetaMask as reported, not fetched-per-wallet.
- **EIP-7702 (Set Code for EOAs) — FINAL, activated in Pectra (May 2025). Enabler *and* the same multisig-signer hazard.** It lets an EOA attach a delegation (`0xef0100 || address`) so its address executes a contract's code in the EOA's context, enabling atomic `approve → buy → stake` from a normal wallet with one signature — the mechanism behind MetaMask's one-confirmation batch. **Same mechanism = the sweeper class:** the spec warns a poorly implemented delegate can allow near-complete control over a signer's EOA, and apps must NEVER ask users to sign raw authorizations. Real-world: InfernoDrainer/PinkDrainer industrialized 7702 sweeper delegations; one victim lost **$1.54M** after a routine-looking Uniswap approval; researchers found the large majority of on-chain 7702 delegations point to sweeper families. The distinction is who you delegate to: the enabler is the user's *own* wallet delegating to an *audited* account they control; the hazard is delegating to an attacker's contract, or a protocol multisig *signer* EOA getting swept. ([eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702) · [ethereum.org/roadmap/pectra/7702](https://ethereum.org/roadmap/pectra/7702/) · [cryptopolitan.com — $1.54M phishing](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/eip-7702-user-loses-1-54m-phishing-attack/) · [zealynx.io — 7702 wallet security](https://www.zealynx.io/research/smart-contracts/eip-7702-wallet-security))
- **ERC-4337 EntryPoint v0.8 (2025) — natively bridges 7702 EOAs into the bundler/paymaster ecosystem.** v0.8 is deployed on mainnet at **`0x4337084D9E255Ff0702461CF8895CE9E3b5Ff108`** — this address is real and verifiable on Etherscan and matches the eth-infinitism v0.8.0 release (prior: v0.6 `0x5FF137D4b0FDCD49DcA30c7CF57E578a026d2789`, v0.7 `0x0000000071727De22E5E9d8BAf0edAc6f37da032`). Standard hygiene still applies: **verify it on Etherscan against the eth-infinitism repo before integrating.** v0.8 adds native EIP-7702 support (UserOp hash includes the 7702 delegation address; `eth_sendUserOperation` takes `eip7702Auth`), native ERC-712 UserOp signatures, and ships **`Simple7702Account`** — a fully audited minimalist smart wallet any EOA can safely authorize. It also relaxed the unused-gas penalty (none below 40k gas) and fixed paymaster `postOp`, zero-address-paymaster, and initCode front-running bugs. **Relevant to Tegridy only if it wants server-sponsored gasless buys or session keys; pure one-click UX needs NO 4337 client code.** ([erc4337.substack.com — v0.8 released](https://erc4337.substack.com/p/entrypoint-v08-released) · [github.com/eth-infinitism/account-abstraction/releases](https://github.com/eth-infinitism/account-abstraction/releases) · [docs.pimlico.io — 4337 vs 7702](https://docs.pimlico.io/guides/eip7702/erc4337-vs-eip7702))
- **Gasless sponsorship: a `paymasterService` capability, standardized in the still-DRAFT ERC-7677.** EIP-5792 defines the generic capability channel and mentions `paymasterService` only as a **non-normative ERC-4337 example** — it does **not** reference ERC-7677. The `paymasterService` semantics (a URL the wallet calls during the batch to sponsor gas) are defined by **ERC-7677, a separate ERC still in DRAFT.** Net effect is still favorable: gas sponsorship rides the same `wallet_sendCalls` capability plumbing with no separate 4337 client — but cite ERC-7677 (draft) as the sponsorship contract, not the EIP-5792 spec.
- **Intents (CoW / UniswapX / 1inch Fusion) — the MEV-protected one-signature path.** The user signs an off-chain order describing the outcome (buy X for ≤ Y ETH); a competitive solver/filler network executes it. UniswapX uses a Dutch auction of fillers; CoW uses batch auctions with coincidence-of-wants and solvers absorb all MEV risk, settling at a uniform clearing price (structurally sandwich-protected); 1inch Fusion is hybrid RFQ+auction. All three run billions monthly in 2026. Fits the **post-graduation open-market buy**, not the Doppler-curve + Afterlife-stake sequence (which is Tegridy-specific and not a native solver market). ([docs.cow.fi — intents](https://docs.cow.fi/cow-protocol/concepts/introduction/intents) · [eco.com — UniswapX 2026 guide](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/11852773-what-is-uniswapx-2026-guide) · [blockeden.xyz — intent architecture](https://blockeden.xyz/forum/t/uniswapx-cow-protocol-and-1inch-fusion-now-handle-billions-in-monthly-volume-through-solver-auctions-the-intent-architecture-explained-for-builders/462))
- **Session keys (ERC-7715 `wallet_grantPermissions` / ERC-7710 delegation) — still DRAFT.** The right primitive for recurring/DCA buys: grant a dapp scoped, time-bounded, spend-limited authority once, then execute within bounds without per-tx prompts. MetaMask ships this via its Delegation Toolkit / Advanced Permissions; Coinbase, Biconomy, Rhinestone build the same shape. **ERC-7715 is still DRAFT as of 2026 (draft since May 2024) — do NOT build a load-bearing flow on it yet.** ([docs.metamask.io — advanced permissions](https://docs.metamask.io/smart-accounts-kit/concepts/advanced-permissions/) · [docs.gator.metamask.io — ERC-7715](https://docs.gator.metamask.io/experimental/erc-7715-request-permissions) · [eco.com — ERC-7715 explained](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/11953354-erc-7715-explained-wallet-permissions-sessions-and-subscriptions))

### Concrete extension of Tegridy's EIP-5792 base

Ship the one-click launch-buy as a **pure additive extension** of the existing base — **zero new Solidity**:

1. **`buildLaunchBuyCalls()`** beside `stakeBatch.ts`'s `buildApproveStakeCalls()`, composing legs that already exist as seams:
   `[approve WETH to curve] → [buy on the Doppler bonding curve] → [stake received tokens via afterlife.ts boosted-LP/gauge] → [optional attestFactSheet() EAS attestation]`.
   Every leg is the signer's **own** action on the signer's **own** funds, visible in one tx. The honest legs already exist: `afterlife.ts` computes boosted-LP staker + gauge eligibility (honestly gated to pending-deployment when a dep is zero-address), and `attestation.ts` writes a revocable EAS fact-sheet attestation (disclosure, not a warranty).
2. **`useOneClickLaunchBuy()`** cloning `useOneClickStake.ts`'s posture: gate on `isAtomicBatchSupported(caps,'0x1')`, keep the sequential flow as fallback, throw-on-reject. It inherits the existing wallet-detection gating verbatim — no new wallet-detection code.
3. **Output-feeds-next dependency:** where the buy's received-token amount feeds the stake leg, keep those two legs sequential (two-signature) *or* use the wallet's dependency handling. **Do NOT hand-encode a novel multicall/wrapper contract** to force atomicity — that is exactly the custom Solidity to avoid.
   - **OPEN:** confirm whether the Doppler curve-buy exposes a single-call buy whose received amount is knowable/boundable up-front. If the stake leg needs the exact received amount, buy+stake stays two-signature (acceptable) rather than gaining a custom wrapper.

### Security scoping (non-negotiable)

- **(a) NEVER build a UI that asks the user to sign a raw 7702 authorization tuple to a Tegridy-supplied address.** That is the documented $1.54M sweeper class. Rely **solely** on the wallet's own `wallet_sendCalls` 7702 mediation.
- **(b) Keep protocol-admin Safe signer keys NON-7702-delegated.** A swept signer EOA = a compromised multisig. Add this to the operator policy alongside the pending Safe-rebuild task. Post-Pectra auditors scope wallet code + delegate + protocol EOA-assumptions as one surface (Zealynx). *(OPEN: confirm current admin signers are EOAs that could be delegated, and document the policy.)*
- **(c) If — and only if — gasless is added:** use a **hosted paymaster** (Pimlico/Alchemy/Biconomy) surfaced through the **draft ERC-7677** `paymasterService` capability — config, not code. Policy: whitelist **exactly** the launch-buy target+selector, per-user/per-window spend cap, and **charge during validation, not `postOp`**. Do NOT write a custom paymaster contract — that re-triggers a full audit *and* is the exact OtterSec-documented drain class: (1) unused-gas-penalty exploitation (attackers inflate gas limits so the EntryPoint penalty drains the paymaster deposit; v0.8 relaxes below 40k gas but the class remains); (2) free sponsorship when `postOp` is forced to revert (the EntryPoint still completes execution and pays the bundler). ([osec.io — paymasters on EVM](https://osec.io/blog/2025-12-02-paymasters-evm/))
- **(d) Defer session keys** until ERC-7715 exits draft; then scope to (target = staking/launch contract, cap = user-set, short expiry) so a leaked key loses only the capped amount. Use the MetaMask Delegation Toolkit rather than hand-rolling a delegation manager.
- **Use CoW/UniswapX intents only for the post-graduation open-market buy** where MEV protection is the point. *(OPEN: confirm solvers route to a freshly-graduated, thin V4 pool vs only established pairs — affects whether intents are viable for brand-new launch tokens or only for TOWELI.)*

### Fork-not-build (Part B)

## Part C — Why NOT wash "volume bots" / multi-wallet "bundlers"

This is the documented basis for declining both requests. The manipulation versions are not a gray area — three independent forces converge: **detection** (discovery surfaces filter the fake volume out, and clustering exposes bundled supply as a permanent on-chain flag), **legal** (criminal convictions, statutory bans, and live class actions), and **reputational** (the marquee cautionary cases plaintiff firms now template).

### Detection — the fake volume buys nothing durable

- **Aggregators discard fake volume.** CoinGecko / GeckoTerminal — the discovery surface Tegridy tokens live on — do not take reported volume at face value. **Trust Score's "Basilisk Update" (May 2026) REMOVED web-traffic normalization entirely and switched to direct trading-volume + order-book-depth grading on a curve versus peers** (plus a new Regulation Score); pairs inactive >3h are dropped. *(Correction to an earlier draft: the "discard reported volume and substitute a web-traffic estimate" logic is the SUPERSEDED pre-Basilisk Trust Score — not Basilisk. The top-level point stands: paid-for volume is filtered out of the very rankings a bot is meant to game.)* DEXScreener surfaces wash patterns as rapid alternating same-size/same-interval trades. Net: the spend is filtered out before it can help discovery, while leaving an on-chain trail that flags the token. ([CoinGecko Trust Score methodology](https://support.coingecko.com/hc/en-us/articles/36442561461657-Trust-Score-Methodology) · [coingecko.com/en/methodology](https://www.coingecko.com/en/methodology) · [cryptoadventure.com — CoinGecko 2026 review](https://cryptoadventure.com/coingecko-review-2026-trust-score-accuracy-portfolio-tracking-and-api-pricing/))
- **Bundled launches are trivially exposed by funding-graph clustering.** Multi-wallet "bundler" launches show up as dozens of medium bubbles (1–5% each) receiving supply from a central deployer in the same block — a Sybil signature **Bubblemaps'** bundle-checker reads off launch-block data, and **Nansen/Arkham** trace every wallet's funding graph (N wallets tracing to one funder or CEX-withdrawal cluster collapse to a single entity). Academic work now catalogs this at scale: **arXiv 2607.02795** identifies **1,012 persistent sniper cohorts across 2,965 unique addresses on pump.fun** via a two-stage cohort-detection pipeline. An "anti-Bubblemaps undetectability" bundler industry exists precisely because detection is the default — an arms race Tegridy would be on the losing and legally-exposed side of. A disclosure-first launcher whose own tokens showed bundled supply would be self-refuting: the same tools it cites for trust would flag its launches. ([blog.bubblemaps.io — analyze meme-coin holders](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/) · [dextools.io — Bubblemaps detect manipulation](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/how-to-use-bubblemaps-detect-token-manipulation-tutorial-2026) · [arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795) · [nansen.ai — suspicious wallet activity](https://www.nansen.ai/post/blockchain-analysis-tools-identifying-suspicious-wallet-activity))

### Legal (US criminal) — the load-bearing precedent

DOJ's **"Operation Token Mirrors"** — an FBI sting using a purpose-built token (**NexFundAI**) — produced the first criminal resolutions against market makers for crypto wash trading, with actual prison time and forfeiture. In Oct 2024, DOJ (D. Mass.) charged **18+ people/entities** for wash trading to inflate crypto volume:

- **Gotbit Consulting + founder Aleksei Andriunin** — guilty pleas (wire fraud + conspiracy); founder **sentenced to ~8 months in prison + 1 year supervised release**, **Gotbit forfeited ~$23M** (and got 5 years' probation), after arrest in Portugal and extradition. ([DOJ — Gotbit plead guilty](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/cryptocurrency-financial-services-firm-gotbit-and-founder-plead-guilty-market) · [DOJ — Gotbit sentenced](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/cryptocurrency-financial-services-firm-gotbit-and-founder-sentenced-market-manipulation) · [decrypt.co — Gotbit founder sentenced](https://decrypt.co/325111/gotbit-founder-sentenced-prison-crypto-wash-trading))
- **CLS Global FZC** — pleaded guilty; **sentenced 2 Apr 2025 to $428,059 + 3 years' probation**, and barred from U.S. crypto markets; it admitted providing "market making services … that included wash trading to fraudulently attract investors." ([DOJ — CLS Global sentenced](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/cryptocurrency-financial-services-firm-sentenced-cryptocurrency-wash-trading))
- **MyTrade (founder Liu Zhou)** — pleaded guilty Oct 2024. ([DOJ — MyTrade plead guilty](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/founder-cryptocurrency-financial-services-firm-mytrade-pleads-guilty-market-manipulation))
- **SEC parallel charges (Press Release 2024-166)** against three market makers (ZM Quant, Gotbit, CLS Global) and nine individuals for wash trading crypto securities. ([DOJ — 18 charged](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ma/pr/eighteen-individuals-and-entities-charged-international-operation-targeting-widespread) · [SEC PR 2024-166](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-166))

**The theory of liability is wire fraud, and it is generic:** manufacturing fake volume to induce purchases is fraud regardless of the wrapper. A "bundler" that splits a buy across freshly-funded wallets to fake distribution and hide concentration is the same deception aimed at buyers. Even as a *tool provider*, shipping this is aiding/abetting exposure.

> The 2026 civil-enforcement thaw does **not** erase this. The SEC voluntarily dismissed several Biden-era *civil* crypto cases in early 2026, but the DOJ **criminal** convictions and prison sentences stand, and the CFTC's fraud/manipulation priorities explicitly retain wash trading. A softer civil posture is not a safe harbor for fraud.

### Legal (US statutory) — CEA §4c(a) + CFTC reach

**CEA Section 4c(a) (codified at 7 U.S.C. §6c)** makes it unlawful to enter or confirm a transaction "of the character of … a wash sale … or a fictitious sale." The derivatives-law "specific intent to avoid a bona-fide position" caveat does not rescue a *designed* self-trade volume bot whose purpose *is* fictitious volume. The CFTC's spot-crypto authority is limited to fraud and manipulation — exactly the box wash trading sits in — e.g. it ordered **Coinbase to pay $6.5M** partly for wash trading / inaccurate volume reporting. SEC market-manipulation authority applies wherever the launched token is deemed a security. ([law.cornell.edu — 7 U.S.C. §6c](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/7/6c) · [uslawexplained.com — wash trading](https://uslawexplained.com/wash_trading) · [CFTC Press Release 8369-21](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8369-21))

### Legal (EU) — MiCA Title VI

**MiCA Article 91** reproduces the MAR Art. 12 manipulation catalogue and adds crypto-specific illustrations. It **prohibits market manipulation functionally** — including transactions that give false or misleading signals as to supply/demand/price, which is where wash trading squarely sits — **though it does not literally enumerate the term "wash trading"** (that is commentators' characterization; the conduct clearly falls within the prohibition). Articles 89–90 prohibit insider dealing and unlawful disclosure; ESMA has issued supervisory guidelines for National Competent Authorities to detect market abuse under MiCA, explicitly tuned to social-media-driven, cross-border token launches — the exact profile of launchpad activity. For any EU-touching launch, a volume bot or an insider-sniped/bundled launch is prohibited market abuse; disclosure-first is the MiCA-compatible posture. ([esma.europa.eu — MiCA market-abuse guidelines](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-issues-supervisory-guidelines-prevent-market-abuse-under-mica) · [mica.wtf — Article 91](https://www.mica.wtf/mica/title-vi-prevention-and-prohibition-of-market-abuse-involving-crypto-assets-art.-86-92/article-91) · [cambridge.org — crypto-asset market abuse under MiCA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-risk-regulation/article/cryptoasset-market-abuse-under-eu-mica/FDC11EC096728B9EF1097A5346F0EF27))
